Declaration of Independence

What Does the Declaration Say?

The Declaration of Independence explains why the thirteen American colonies separated from Great Britain. It states that people have rights that governments are created to protect.

The document says that people are created equal and have rights including life, liberty, and the pursuit of happiness. These ideas became an important part of the founding principles of the United States.

Read the Declaration of Independence transcript from the National Archives.

Why Were They Upset With the King?

The Declaration lists complaints against King George III. The colonists believed the king had repeatedly violated their rights and abused his authority.

  1. He interfered with laws the colonists believed were necessary for the public good.
  2. He interfered with colonial governments and legislatures.
  3. He maintained military forces among the colonies during times of peace.

Where Is the Declaration Today?

The original Declaration of Independence is preserved at the National Archives in Washington, D.C.
